We Killed Seasons, 2026
Multichannel video, sound stereo




If you are here, it is because you have the password to access these videos. Each video corresponds to one channel of We Killed Seasons. Conceptually, I divided the channels accordingly to the five characters: (1) the translator reflecting on sensory memories, (2) the one wandering across rooms, (3) the two characters discussing the changing meaning of the sun and the weather, and (4) the dancer.

This is a proposition, and the configuration can change according to the exhibition context, the architecture of the space, and the nature of the event.

Please note that I am still working on this version, particularly the dancer's scene, whose duration will be extended.















How has the representation of the sun changed throughout time and history? Something fundamental has shifted. Five people, disconnected from one another, inhabit a place that seems familiar, yet profoundly transformed by a disturbance in the cosmological order. Bathed in sunlight streaming through skylights and windows, the film installation intertwines reflections on labour, mythological narratives, scientific observations. They remain disconnected from one another, each embodying a different experience on a world where time becomes incrisingly homogeneous.

I chose to shoot the film entirely indoors, so as to not evoke one season that might define this place. The loss natural cycle is a metaphor for our non-stop economic system, continuous labour, and the erosion of fundamental rhythms that results from it. Inevitably, We Killed Seasons makes an allusion to our increasingly warming planet, as our constant activity and our environment are inseparable from one another.


By Lorenza Kaib

"We Killed Seasons” (2026) imagines a world in which seasons no longer exist — the title suggests that we have “killed” them. The transformations of our world through climate change appear here not only on a spatial level but also on a temporal one. The place itself still exists but is fundamentally changed and partly no longer recognizable.In the film, two people engage in a loose conversation about what things used to be like, how things came to this point, and what the situation is now. There is also another narrative voice that attempts to frame everything. In calm scenes, the protagonists are seen interacting with their surroundings. A dead wasp, a pile of leaves, a whale: all appearing in places and situations that feel unnatural, removed from their original context. Five protagonists appear in total, each of them alone. Only the two people who are speaking to one another are connected. Otherwise, there are no encounters, reinforcing the impression of increasing isolation.

When everything is synchronized, it feels like everything is working. Something arrhythmic, and all is out of balance (excerpt from the film).


The sun, the changing meanings it has acquired throughout history, and the continued recognition of its power are among the subjects discussed by the film's protagonists. From there, they speak about the weather, rhythms, and myths. According to Greek mythology, the origin of the seasons lies in an agreement between the gods. This agreement was made after Hades abducted Persephone, the daughter of Demeter. It was decided that Persephone would spend part of the year with her mother Demeter and the other part with Hades in the underworld. With her coming and going, life on Earth also changes: plants grow and wither, and the length of the day increases and decreases.


Arseneau explores the loss of temporal points of reference. The film moves between longing, loneliness, and the subtle breakdown of the psyche. The disappearance of the seasons becomes a metaphor for the elimination of essential human rhythms—a loss of natural cycles that makes the uninterrupted exploitation of resources and labour, around the clock, possible.


- Lorenza Kaib, for the publication SOLASTALGIE – Waldbaden mit Plastikdelfin, 2026 [my translation]
